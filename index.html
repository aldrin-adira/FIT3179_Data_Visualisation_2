Pet Ownership in Australia

Why Australians welcome pets into their homes.

From a worldwide bond to the animals sharing Australian homes, explore the appeal of companionship and the circumstances that shape pet ownership.

01

A worldwide bond

How does Australia compare?

Pet ownership connects households around the world. Begin by placing Australia alongside the 42 other countries with recent national surveys of how many households own a dog or a cat. Most are in Europe; the rest of the world has no comparable survey.

From the international picture, we turn to how pet ownership in Australia has changed over time.

02

More homes, more companions

How has pet ownership changed over time?

Explore both the proportion of Australian households with pets and the number of pet-owning households. Together, these measures help distinguish changing ownership rates from growth in the household population.

But owning a pet can mean very different kinds of companionship.

03

Who shares our homes?

Which animals do Australians keep?

Compare dogs, cats, fish, birds and smaller companion animals through household ownership and the number of animals kept. Households may own several species, so their ownership percentages can overlap.

The next question is what people seek in a companion, and how that fits their circumstances.

04

A companion that fits

Why do Australians get pets, and what influences their choice?

Explore reported motivations, including companionship and wellbeing, alongside differences between household groups. These comparisons describe associations; they do not establish what causes people to choose a particular pet.

Finding a suitable companion also means being able to afford its care.

05

The price of companionship

What financial commitment accompanies different pets?

Consider the household costs of caring for dogs and cats, alongside reported barriers to obtaining a pet. These measures bring the practical commitment of ownership into focus.

Those decisions take place in different local environments across Australia.

06

One country, different local circumstances

How does the picture change when we zoom into particular regions?

Zoom in from the national picture to the 68 councils of South Australia, where dogs and cats are recorded in the state's Dogs and Cats Online system. These records count animals that owners have recorded, so differences between councils also reflect how fully owners comply, not only how many pets they keep.

Local differences sit within a shared national story of companionship and long-term care.

07

A pet-loving nation

What does Australia's enthusiasm for pets require of us?

Bring together the evidence on ownership, motivations, costs and local circumstances to consider what makes lasting companionship possible.

Australia's pet story is one of companionship. Almost three in four homes now have a pet, almost half have a dog (more than in most countries compared), and owners mostly say they want company, calm and better mental health. Whether that companionship can last depends on practical conditions: the cost of care, a home that suits a pet, and the time and responsibility it needs. Choosing a pet that fits the household is what makes companionship possible for the long term.

Datasets

Animal Medicines Australia, Pets in Australia 2025: National online survey by SEC Newgate Research of 2,450 adults (25 February – 10 March 2025), weighted to Australian Bureau of Statistics Census data. Used in Sections 1–5.

International household surveys: European Pet Food Industry Federation, Facts and Figures 2026 (40 European countries, mostly 2024 data); Companion Animals New Zealand, 2024 report; United Kingdom Pet Food, pet population 2026; American Pet Products Association, 2025 report (2024 data). Separate national surveys with different methods. Used in Section 1.

Government of South Australia, Dogs and Cats Online data 2024–2025: Dogs and cats recorded at home in each council, snapshot of 30 May 2025, published by the Department for Environment and Water. Used in Section 6.

Australian Bureau of Statistics, Regional population 2025: Estimated resident population and boundaries of South Australian local government areas, 30 June 2025. Used in Section 6 for rates per 1,000 residents and the maps.

Notes

Sections 1 to 5 use estimates from household surveys, not counts. In Section 1, each country's figure comes from its own national survey, with different methods and years. For its full sample, the Animal Medicines Australia report gives a maximum error margin of plus or minus 2.0 percentage points at the 95% confidence level; the margin is larger for smaller groups, such as small mammal and reptile owners. Percentages may not add to 100 because households can keep several types of pet, owners could give several reasons, and values are rounded. Every number can be checked against the project data files. Section 6 uses council records instead, which count only the animals owners have recorded, so differences between councils partly reflect how fully owners comply.
