More homes, more companions

How has pet ownership changed over time?

Explore both the proportion of Australian households with pets and the number of pet-owning households. Together, these measures help distinguish changing ownership rates from growth in the household population.

But owning a pet can mean very different kinds of companionship.

03

Who shares our homes?

Which animals do Australians keep?

Compare dogs, cats, fish, birds and smaller companion animals through household ownership and the number of animals kept. Households may own several species, so their ownership percentages can overlap.

The next question is what people seek in a companion, and how that fits their circumstances.

04

A companion that fits

Why do Australians get pets, and what influences their choice?

Explore reported motivations, including companionship and wellbeing, alongside differences between household groups. These comparisons describe associations; they do not establish what causes people to choose a particular pet.

Datasets

Animal Medicines Australia, Pets in Australia 2025: National online survey by SEC Newgate Research of 2,450 adults (25 February – 10 March 2025), weighted to Australian Bureau of Statistics Census data. Used in Sections 2–4.

Notes

The figures are estimates from a household survey, not counts. For the full sample the report gives a maximum error margin of plus or minus 2.0 percentage points at the 95% confidence level; the margin is larger for smaller groups, such as small mammal and reptile owners. Percentages may not add to 100 because households can keep several types of pet, owners could give several reasons, and values are rounded. Every number can be checked against the project data files.
